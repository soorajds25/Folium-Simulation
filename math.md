# MATHEMATICAL AND PHYSICS MODEL ARCHITECTURE SPECIFICATION

This document outlines the underlying mathematical framework of the optimization environment. The system models vehicle dynamics in the distance-domain ($s$-domain) to eliminate clock-time dependencies and ensure numerical stability over complex track layouts.

---

## 1. Core Vehicle Dynamics & Forces Framework

The longitudinal acceleration of the vehicle at any spatial position $s$ along the track centerline is governed by Newton's Second Law, adjusted for rotational powertrain component inertia via a dimensionless mass factor[cite: 9]:

$$\frac{dv}{dt} = \frac{\Sigma F_t(s) - \Sigma F_{tr}(s)}{\delta \cdot M_v}$$

Where $v$ is the longitudinal velocity ($\text{m/s}$), $\Sigma F_t$ is the tractive force generated by the powertrain ($\text{N}$), $\Sigma F_{tr}$ is the total resistive force ($\text{N}$), $M_v$ is the total combined mass of the vehicle and driver ($\text{kg}$), and $\delta$ is the dimensionless mass factor capturing rotating powertrain components[cite: 3, 9]. 

To eliminate temporal cross-dependencies, the state equation is explicitly mapped to the distance domain ($s$-domain) using the spatial transformation chain rule:

$$\frac{dv}{dt} = \frac{dv}{ds} \frac{ds}{dt} = v \frac{dv}{ds}$$

$$m \cdot v \cdot \frac{dv}{ds} = F_{\text{trac}}(s) - F_{\text{res}}(v, \theta, \kappa)$$

### 1.1 Aerodynamic Drag Force Model
The aerodynamic drag resistance accounts for vehicle cross-sectional performance, ambient air density, and external wind speed vectors projected onto the vehicle's instantaneous heading[cite: 3, 9]:

$$F_w(s) = \frac{1}{2} \rho_a \cdot C_D A_f \cdot \left( v(s) + v_w \cos(\phi_{\text{veh}}(s) - \phi_{\text{wind}}) \right)^2$$

Where:
*   $\rho_a$: Ambient air density ($\text{kg/m}^3$)[cite: 9].
*   $C_D A_f$: Effective frontal drag area ($\text{m}^2$).
*   $v_w$: Ambient wind speed velocity ($\text{m/s}$)[cite: 9].
*   $\phi_{\text{veh}}(s)$: Vehicle heading angle relative to true North ($\text{rad}$).
*   $\phi_{\text{wind}}$: Absolute vector direction of incoming wind ($\text{rad}$).

### 1.2 Rolling Resistance Model
The tire rolling resistance models energy dissipation through hysteretic carcass deformation over concrete, asphalt, or unpaved tracks[cite: 9]:

$$F_r(s) = M_v \cdot g \cdot f_r(v) \cdot \cos(\theta(s))$$

The rolling resistance coefficient $f_r$ is captured as a velocity-dependent parameter to prevent underestimating power losses at high cruise speeds[cite: 9]:

$$f_r(v) = f_0 + f_s \left( \frac{3.6 \cdot v(s)}{100} \right)^{2.5}$$

Where $f_0$ and $f_s$ are parameters determined by tire structure and inflation pressure[cite: 9]. For computational efficiency within typical Shell Eco-Marathon speed bands ($\le 128 \text{ km/h}$), a localized linear relationship may be substituted[cite: 9]:

$$f_r(v) = 0.01 \cdot \left(1 + \frac{3.6 \cdot v(s)}{100}\right)$$

### 1.3 Gradient (Gravitational) Resistance Model
The slope-dependent force component acting parallel to the road surface plane is modeled directly from track vertical layout properties[cite: 9]:

$$F_g(s) = M_v \cdot g \cdot \sin(\theta(s))$$

Where $\theta(s) = \arctan\left(\frac{dH(s)}{ds}\right)$, with $H(s)$ representing local track elevation in meters[cite: 9].

### 1.4 Axle Normal Load Distribution
To evaluate dynamic friction boundaries, normal forces acting on the front and rear wheel axles are tracked continuously, accounting for mass shift under acceleration and track incline[cite: 9]:

$$W_f(s) = \frac{L_b}{L} M_v g \cos(\theta(s)) - \frac{h_g}{L} \left( F_{\text{trac}}(s) - F_r(s) \left( 1 - \frac{r_d}{h_g} \right) \right)$$

$$W_r(s) = \frac{L_a}{L} M_v g \cos(\theta(s)) + \frac{h_g}{L} \left( F_{\text{trac}}(s) - F_r(s) \left( 1 - \frac{r_d}{h_g} \right) \right)$$

Where $L_a$ and $L_b$ are spatial distances from the vehicle center of gravity to the front and rear axles, respectively, $L$ is the total vehicle wheelbase ($L = L_a + L_b$), $h_g$ is the vertical height of the center of gravity, and $r_d$ is the effective wheel radius[cite: 9].

## 2. Tire-Ground Adhesion and Cornering Boundaries

The maximum tractive effort that can be safely transmitted to the track surface without causing the tire to break traction is limited by the local road adhesion coefficient $\mu$ and normal axle load[cite: 9]:

$$F_{\text{tmax}}(s) = \mu \cdot W_{\text{driven}}(s)$$

### 2.1 Lateral Acceleration and Grip Circle Constraints
When cornering, the tire must negotiate a localized lateral acceleration vector dictated by track curvature $\kappa(s)$ and velocity:

$$a_{\text{lat}}(s) = v(s)^2 \cdot \kappa(s) = \frac{v(s)^2}{r_{\text{corner}}(s)}$$

To prevent vehicle slipping or rollover, combined tractive and lateral force components must fall within the boundary specified by the friction circle envelope:

$$\left( \frac{F_{\text{trac}}(s)}{W_{\text{driven}}(s)} \right)^2 + \left( \frac{M_v \cdot v(s)^2 \cdot \kappa(s)}{W_{\text{total}}(s)} \right)^2 \le \mu_{\text{lat}}^2$$

This yields an explicit mathematical velocity limit boundary at any given coordinate node on the track:

$$v_{\text{bound}}(s) = \sqrt{\frac{\mu_{\text{lat}} \cdot g}{\kappa(s)}} = \sqrt{\mu_{\text{lat}} \cdot g \cdot r_{\text{corner}}(s)}$$

## 3. Powertrain Component & Efficiency Models

### 3.1 Drivetrain Geometry Transformation
Wheel speed angular velocity ($\omega_w$) and mechanical tractive demand are mapped back to the motor output shaft via the fixed drivetrain gear ratio $G$ and wheel radius $r_w$:

$$\Omega_{\text{motor}}(s) = \frac{v(s)}{r_w} \cdot G \cdot \frac{60}{2\pi} \quad (\text{RPM})$$

$$T_{\text{motor}}(s) = \frac{F_{\text{trac}}(s) \cdot r_w}{G \cdot \eta_{\text{trans}}}$$

Where $\eta_{\text{trans}}$ represents the mechanical efficiency of the transmission system components (chain, bearings, or gears)[cite: 3, 9].

### 3.2 Data-Driven Motor Efficiency Mapping
Electrical power requirements ($P_{\text{elec}}$) are calculated by interpolating a 3D dyno-sourced efficiency map ($\eta_m$) matching the current operating torque and rotational velocity profile:

$$P_{\text{elec}}(s) = \begin{cases} 
\frac{T_{\text{motor}}(s) \cdot \Omega_{\text{motor}}(s) \cdot \left(\frac{2\pi}{60}\right)}{\eta_m(T_{\text{motor}}, \Omega_{\text{motor}})} & \text{if } F_{\text{trac}} \ge 0 \quad (\text{Propulsion / Burst}) \\
T_{\text{motor}}(s) \cdot \Omega_{\text{motor}}(s) \cdot \left(\frac{2\pi}{60}\right) \cdot \eta_{\text{gen}}(T_{\text{motor}}, \Omega_{\text{motor}}) & \text{if } F_{\text{trac}} < 0 \quad (\text{Regenerative Braking})
\end{cases}$$

### 3.3 Battery Terminal State Equations
The terminal discharge voltage ($V_t$) and current draw ($I_{\text{batt}}$) are derived using an internal resistance circuit model[cite: 9]:

$$I_{\text{batt}}(s) = \frac{V_{oc}(\text{SOC}) - \sqrt{V_{oc}(\text{SOC})^2 - 4 \cdot R_{\text{int}} \cdot P_{\text{elec}}(s)}}{2 \cdot R_{\text{int}}}$$

$$V_t(s) = V_{oc}(\text{SOC}) - I_{\text{batt}}(s) \cdot R_{\text{int}}$$

Where $V_{oc}$ is the open-circuit voltage mapped to the battery's State of Charge (SOC), and $R_{\text{int}}$ is the localized internal resistance of the battery cells[cite: 9].

## 4. Trajectory Optimization Formulations

The core optimization challenge aims to minimize total electrical energy consumption across a complete competition run.

### 4.1 Objective Cost Function ($J$)
The multi-objective trajectory performance cost function is structured as follows:

$$J = \int_{0}^{S_{\text{target}}} \left[ w_E \cdot P_{\text{elec}}(u(s), v(s)) \cdot \frac{1}{v(s)} + w_{\Delta u} \left( \frac{du}{ds} \right)^2 \right] ds + w_T \cdot T_{\text{lap}} + \Phi_{\text{terminal}}$$

Where $u(s) \in [0, 1]$ represents the continuous throttle control variable, $w_E$ is the primary energy weighting coefficient, $w_{\Delta u}$ acts as a smoothing regularizer to suppress rapid control chatter, and $w_T$ is a penalty parameter tuned iteratively to ensure compliance with the target lap time.

### 4.2 System Physical & Competition Constraints
$$\text{Subject to: } \quad v(s) \le v_{\text{bound}}(s) \quad (\text{Cornering Grip Bound}) \text{}$$

$$I_{\text{batt}}(s) \le I_{\text{max}} \quad (\text{Battery Overcurrent Fuse Limit}) \text{[cite: 7]}$$

$$V_{\text{min}} \le V_t(s) \le V_{\text{max}} \quad (\text{Voltage Safety Bounds}) \text{[cite: 7]}$$

$$\Omega_{\text{motor}}(s) \le \Omega_{\text{max}} \quad (\text{Motor Physical Redline Limit}) \text{}$$

$$\int_{0}^{S_{\text{target}}} \frac{1}{v(s)} ds \le T_{\text{target}} \quad (\text{Official Competition Lap Time Limit}) \text{}$$
